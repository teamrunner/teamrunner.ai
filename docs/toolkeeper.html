A toolkeeper

A toolkeeper is the member who holds a tool: a web search, a page reader, a Python sandbox, a mailbox. It has no model and no prompt. Other members ask it during their turns; it checks who is asking, keeps to its terms, does the work and logs every request. No other member ever holds a tool. A toolkeeper is a member file like any other, with kind: toolkeeper in its settings.

A first toolkeeper

Give the debate's fact checker the web. Add web.md to the team:

---
description: The web toolkeeper — searches the web, on different terms for different members
kind: toolkeeper
tools:
  web_search_brave: {max_results: 5, calls_per_turn: 2}   # the base terms
access:
  fact_checker: {calls_per_turn: 3}                 # its own terms
  judge: standard                                   # the base terms
---
I search the web. Send me a search query and I return the top five
results with their sources.

It holds one tool, web_search_brave, with its base terms: five results a search, two searches a turn. Two members may use it: the fact checker on terms of its own (three searches a turn, and the base five results), the judge on the standard terms. Everyone else is refused. The text below the settings is the sign on the door: what the fact checker and the judge are told about it. Tell the fact checker it may ask, in fact_checker.md:

To check a claim you cannot confirm from what you know, search the web:
ask the web toolkeeper.

Searches go through Brave Search, so a real run needs BRAVE_API_KEY in your key file. Try it first, spending nothing:

teamrunner validate debate --offline # the files onlyteamrunner explain debate # who may use web, on what termsteamrunner run debate --rehearse # the flow, with requests checked but no search made

explain prints the terms in plain words: fact_checker may use it: 3 calls a turn, max results 5; judge may use it: 2 calls a turn, max results 5; others: no access. A shipped example has two toolkeepers, one reading web pages and one running Python: teamrunner new my_debate --from fact_checked. Another has three, on three sets of terms: --from reference_check.

The rest of this page is every setting in detail.

The settings block

A toolkeeper's file starts with its settings between two lines of ---, like any member's. Its name is its file name, without .md and without a leading number (01_web.md is web), so name it after what it offers: web.md, sandbox.md, library.md. That is how its teammates address it.

Setting Needed What it is
kind yes toolkeeper
tools yes The one tool it holds, with its base terms
access no Who may use it, one line per member, and on what terms
others no What everyone not listed in access: gets; refused if absent
description no One line for people
id and parent_id written for you Who this file is, across edits and copies

A toolkeeper has no model:, params: or output:: each is refused, because a toolkeeper decides by its access: table, with no model. Any other word is refused with the nearest known word (acess → did you mean access?).

kind:

kind: toolkeeper. Without it a file is a member with a model (kind: llm, the default). kind: is one word: a list is refused. The old word kind: gate is refused with what replaced it. A misspelled kind is refused, saying the kinds are llm and toolkeeper. A wrong shape here is a problem line naming the file and line, not a Python error. New in 0.0.7.

tools:

The tool the toolkeeper holds, by its name, and under it the base terms: what every member on standard terms gets.

tools:
  read_page: {domains: [api.crossref.org], max_chars: 20000, calls_per_turn: 10}

The terms can also go on lines of their own, indented under the tool. {} means no terms of your own: the defaults below apply.

  • One tool. A toolkeeper holds one tool, so its terms always mean one thing. Two tools are refused, saying to split the file into one toolkeeper per tool. A member who needs two tools asks two toolkeepers.
  • A built-in tool. The name must be one of the built-in tools: web_search_brave, read_page, read_file, run_code, read_email, save_draft. A misspelled one is refused with the nearest name (web_serch → did you mean web_search_brave?).
  • Its own terms only. Each tool takes the general terms and some of its own. A term the tool does not take is refused, naming the ones it does (read_file has no guardrail domains). The old word max_calls is refused: it is now calls_per_turn.
  • Values of the right kind. A term with a value of the wrong kind is refused at its line: calls_per_turn: lots (a whole number, 1 or more), network: yes (true or false), domains: example.org (a list in brackets), only_input: [a] (one name, not a list). New in 0.0.7.
  • A toolkeeper with no tools: is refused. So are tools: left empty, a list (tools: [web_search_brave]), and a tool with nothing after its colon: write {}.
  • Only toolkeepers have tools:. A member with a model that has tools:, access: or others: is refused: give the tool to a toolkeeper and let the member ask it.

access:

Who may use the toolkeeper, one line per member: the member's name, then one of three values.

You write It means
judge: standard The judge may use it on the base terms in tools:
fact_checker: {calls_per_turn: 3} The fact checker may use it; the terms listed replace those in tools:, and the rest stay as they are there
advocate: deny The advocate is refused
  • Anyone not listed is refused, unless others: says otherwise. So deny is rarely needed: it says out loud that a member is shut out, or shuts out one member when others: lets everyone else in.
  • Changed terms merge with the base. In the first example the fact checker's line changes only calls_per_turn; it still gets max_results: 5 from tools:. A changed term may be stricter or looser than the base; TeamRunner does not compare them.
  • Names are written out. There are no patterns or wildcards, so a new member never gains access by accident. The order of the lines does not matter.
  • Each name must be a member of this team, by its file name. A name that is not is refused with the nearest one (jduge → did you mean judge?). A toolkeeper may not list itself, and may not list another toolkeeper: toolkeepers do not ask each other.
  • A value that is not one of the three (judge: allow) is refused. Changed terms are checked as the terms under tools: are.
  • No one at all. A toolkeeper with no access: and no others: is a warning, "no one may use this toolkeeper", not an error.

others:

What every member not listed in access: gets: standard, changed terms in braces, or deny. Without it, they are refused. It sits beside access:, not inside it.

tools:
  web_search_brave: {}
others: standard          # everyone may search, five calls a turn
access:
  advocate: deny          # except the advocate

A toolkeeper open to everyone with no limits of its own is still a member: every request still goes through it and is logged. Because others, standard and deny are words this file uses, no member may be named others.md, standard.md or deny.md.

description:

One line of text for people, such as The web toolkeeper — searches the web. The members who ask the toolkeeper are shown its body, not this.

id:

The first time you validate or run, TeamRunner writes an id: line at the top of the settings, with a comment: it keeps this toolkeeper's history together. It stays the same when you edit, rename or copy the file. parent_id: is written by teamrunner new --from and says which file this one was copied from. Leave both as they are.

The terms

Each term goes under the tool in tools: (the base), on a member's line in access:, or in others:.

Term Tools Default
calls_per_turn all 5
calls_per_run all no limit
timeout all 60 seconds
max_results web_search_brave 10, and at most 20
keep_results web_search_brave false
domains web_search_brave, read_page any site
max_chars read_page, read_file, read_email, save_draft 50,000 characters
types read_page, read_file any kind it can read
network run_code false
paths run_code none: only the run's work folder
mailbox read_email, save_draft mailbox, in the package
only_input read_email any email in the mailbox
reply_only save_draft true

TeamRunner keeps the terms, not the member: the member is never shown them (unless the sign on the door says so) and simply hears "Refused: …" when it goes past one. Every request records the terms that applied.

calls_per_turn:

How many requests one member may make to this toolkeeper in one turn: a whole number, 1 or more. Written or not, it always applies, so a side trip always ends. The count is per member, like a library card. A request past it is refused, "limit reached: 2 calls a turn", the member is told, and the turn carries on.

calls_per_run:

How many requests one member may make to this toolkeeper in the whole run: a whole number, 1 or more. No limit if absent. Use it to ask a research question ("ten searches in total: who uses them?"). A request past it is refused, "limit reached: 10 calls a run".

timeout:

How long one request may take, in seconds: a number above 0 (10, 2.5). Default 60. A request that takes longer is refused, "it took longer than 10 seconds".

max_results:

How many results a search returns: a whole number, 1 or more. Default 10. Brave returns at most 20 a search, so a larger number gives 20.

keep_results:

true or false, default false. Brave's terms allow storing search results only on plans with storage rights. So by default the member reads the results, but the run's log keeps only the query and how many came back. keep_results: true keeps the results too, for whoever has such a plan.

domains:

The sites the tool may reach, as a list: domains: [api.crossref.org]. A site includes its subdomains (example.org lets in www.example.org). For read_page, a link to any other site is refused, and so is a link that redirects to one. For web_search_brave, the search is limited to those sites and any other result is dropped. The list may not be empty.

max_chars:

The most characters a reply holds: a whole number, 1 or more. Default 50,000. A longer page, file or email is cut there, with a note saying where. For save_draft it limits the draft instead: a longer draft is refused, not cut.

types:

The kinds of file the tool will open, as a list. pdf is the only kind so far, so types: [pdf] takes PDFs only, and anything else is refused, "not a PDF". Without it, the tool reads any kind it can: text, HTML and PDF. Use it when only the full paper will do, and let the flow decide what happens when it is not one (reader --not_full--> stop, in the reference_check example).

network:

true or false, default false. Off, the code run_code runs cannot reach any network. On, the code's fetch() reaches the internet only: TeamRunner makes the request, with read_page's rule, and never reaches this computer, your local network (IPv6 included) or a cloud machine's metadata address, redirects included; replies over 25 MB are refused. Only fetch() reaches the internet; the code has no other network. New in 0.0.7.

paths:

Folders run_code may read, as a list: paths: [data]. A folder written without / or ~ is found from the top of the package. Inside the sandbox, a folder in the package appears at /package/<its path> (/package/data), and one outside it at /outside/<its name>. The folders are copied in, at most 100 MB. A folder that does not exist refuses the request. In the browser page, a folder outside the folder you opened is refused.

mailbox:

The folder of emails read_email reads and save_draft answers: one name, not a list. Default mailbox, found from the top of the package. Each email is one .eml file there, and its id is the file name without .eml (m001.eml is m001). A mailbox that does not exist refuses the request. In the browser page, a mailbox outside the folder you opened is refused. New in 0.0.7.

only_input:

The name of one of the team's inputs: only_input: email_id. read_email then hands over only the email whose id this run was given in that input, and refuses any other. It must name an input a prompt uses as {{email_id}}; a name no prompt uses is refused with the nearest one.

reply_only:

true or false, default true. With true, a draft goes only to the sender of the email it answers (its Reply-To, else its From); a draft addressed to anyone else is refused. With false, the member may name another address.

The built-in tools

Every tool ships with TeamRunner, so a toolkeeper copied into another team runs there unchanged. Each takes the general terms (calls_per_turn, calls_per_run, timeout) and its own.

web_search_brave

A query in; titles, links and snippets out, from Brave Search. Its own terms: max_results, domains, keep_results. It needs a Brave Search API key, BRAVE_API_KEY, in ~/.teamrunner/.env; plain teamrunner validate looks for it. A search costs $0.005 at Brave's list price, and a run's cost counts it, with models and tools shown apart. A run's --max-cost sets the search's price aside before it is made, and refuses it, "the run's cost ceiling is reached", when it does not fit. New in 0.0.7. A different price can go in ~/.teamrunner/prices.yaml.

read_page

A link in; the page's text out. It reads web pages, online PDFs and plain text. Its own terms: domains, max_chars, types. It needs no key. Links must start with http:// or https://. It never reads this computer or its local network, even through a redirect, and refuses a page larger than 25 MB or a kind of file it cannot read. When a site answers with an error, the member is told the code (The site answered 404 (Not Found).).

read_file

A file name in; its text out. It reads only the files this run was given, with teamrunner run --file, and refuses anything else, naming the files it has. Its own terms: max_chars, types.

run_code

Python code in; what it prints, its warnings and its errors out, and the names of any files it left. The code runs in a sandbox: Python in WebAssembly, under Deno, which installs with TeamRunner (Pyodide is downloaded the first time). It can use packages built for Pyodide, such as numpy, pandas, scipy and sympy, but not every package. It writes only in the run's own work folder, runs/<run>/work/, where its files stay for the rest of the run. Files it leaves there are capped at 100 MB. New in 0.0.7. Its own terms: network, paths. Its reply is cut at 50,000 characters.

read_email

An email's id in; its sender, recipients, date, subject, text and the names of any attachments out. Its own terms: mailbox, max_chars, only_input.

save_draft

The id of the email it answers and the reply's text in (and, optionally, who it goes to). The draft is saved as runs/<run>/work/outbox/<id>.eml, for a person to read. Nothing is ever sent. One draft per email: a second is refused. Its own terms: mailbox, max_chars, reply_only.

The body: the sign on the door

Everything below the second --- is what every member who may use the toolkeeper is told about it, so it knows what to ask for. A member's body is its private prompt; a toolkeeper's body is posted on the door. Say what to send and what comes back: "Send me a link to a PDF and I return its text." It can also guide good use, as the fact_checked example's web.md does with the Crossref links to try.

It is not a prompt, and nothing reads it but the members: {{…}} in it is not filled in. Members who may not use the toolkeeper never see it.

How members ask it

A member asks a toolkeeper by calling it, the way a model calls a tool: each toolkeeper that would let the member in appears to the member's model as a tool named after it (web), described by its sign on the door. The request is that tool's own arguments (a query, a link, the code), never free text that TeamRunner reads for a request. The reply goes only to whoever asked. The request, the toolkeeper's yes or no and the result are all logged, as the toolkeeper's.

There are two ways to ask.

A side trip

Nothing in the flow mentions the toolkeeper. During its own turn a member may ask it as often as it needs, or not at all, up to its calls_per_turn, then finishes its turn. The reply comes back inside that turn, to that member alone. This is how the fact checker uses web in the first example. A member may ask any number of toolkeepers that let it in.

A step in the flow

When asking must happen, put the toolkeeper in chair.md:

rounds(*3) -> judge:research -> web -> judge:verdict

The member just before the toolkeeper's step must end its turn with a request to it; that turn's answer is the request, so it gives no typed outputs. A turn that ends without one is asked again, up to three tries. The toolkeeper then checks, makes the request and replies to that member alone, who reads the reply at its next step. If others need the result, the asker passes it on. The request counts as one call in the asker's turn. A refused request is logged, the asker is told, and the flow carries on. What the flow may not do with a toolkeeper is on the chair's page.

In a rehearsal, every request is checked against access: and the limits, but no tool runs: the member gets placeholder words, at no cost and with no key.

What validate refuses

Each of these is an error at the line it names, before anything is spent:

  • kind: that is not one word, a misspelled kind, or kind: gate (now kind: toolkeeper).
  • model:, params: or output: on a toolkeeper; a word a member file does not know.
  • No tools:; tools: empty, a list, or with nothing after the tool's name; two tools; a tool that is not built in.
  • A term the tool does not take, max_calls, or a value of the wrong kind.
  • In access:, a name that is not a member of this team, the toolkeeper itself, another toolkeeper, or a value other than standard, deny or terms in braces. The same for others:.
  • only_input: naming an input no prompt uses.
  • tools:, access: or others: on a member that is not a toolkeeper.
  • A tools/ folder in the package.
  • What looks like an API key in a team's files, Brave keys included. New in 0.0.7.

One warning: a toolkeeper no one may use.

Not yet: a team's own tools in a tools/ folder, and access for a whole subteam by its name, come in a later version of TeamRunner.
