Step 6Tools, through a toolkeeper

follow in

Agents never hold tools themselves. A tool sits with a toolkeeper: a member with no model that checks who is asking and enforces the limits, like the attendant at a tool crib. In checked, web.md holds the web, and its access: list says who may use it:

checked/web.mdthe whole file
---
description: The web toolkeeper — reads public research records and source pages
kind: toolkeeper
tools:
  read_page: {max_chars: 20000, calls_per_turn: 2}   # no search-service key needed
access:
  fact_checker: {calls_per_turn: 3}
  judge: standard
---
I read public web pages and research records. Send me a URL and I return
its text. The fact checker gets three reads a turn, the judge two.

To find research, use Crossref's public API, with URL-encoded words:
https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=remote+work+productivity&rows=3
Change the query to match the claim. Records may include abstracts,
DOIs and source links; follow those links to check the evidence itself.
You can also send a known public source URL. Do not invent source URLs.
A title or search match alone is not evidence that a claim is true.
If a page is unavailable or the evidence is insufficient, say so.

Above the second --- are its settings: one tool, read_page, and the access: list. Below them are its instructions, which go to whoever asks. If a member not on the list asks, the request is refused. TeamRunner enforces these terms, so you don’t rely on the agents behaving well. A lookup is a side trip inside the asker’s own turn, not a step in the flow, so chair.md doesn’t change.

The fact checker may use it three times a turn; the judge on the standard terms; the debaters, not listed, argue from what they know. Give the side arguing for the web too, once a turn, and the debate is no longer even:

checked/web.mdadd a line
access:
+ for: {calls_per_turn: 1}

Save. The plain-words summary under Flow diagram says who may use what, on which terms. Then rehearse.

explain prints who may use what, on which terms:

teamrunner explain checked teamrunner run checked --rehearse

Every request, the toolkeeper's yes or no, and what came back are kept in the run. checked also has a second toolkeeper, sandbox.md, which runs Python so the fact checker can check the sums in a claim.
